# Reader Flow Review

Use this skill to critique or revise prose by protecting the reader's path through the piece. The goal is a useful editorial intervention, not a house style.

## Response Shape

1. State the dominant reader issue first. If the passage already works for the stated purpose, say that and recommend no change or a minimal edit.
2. Explain the reader effect in one concrete sentence.
3. Give one to three options keyed to the writer's purpose or audience. Name the tradeoff and edit scope for each option.
4. Revise only the requested span or the smallest span needed to solve the stated issue.
5. End with preservation notes and claim notes when relevant.

## Editorial Judgments

- Put familiar ground before new terms. If a reader must learn a coined term, system label, or abstraction, anchor it first in the reader's known question, problem, or scene.
- Lead with the issue that can change the writer's decision. Evidence gaps, unsupported conclusions, audience mismatch, and broken orientation outrank grammar polish.
- Fit options to purpose. A practitioner note, graduate essay, release note, and fiction excerpt do different jobs; do not collapse them into one generic serious style.
- Preserve working voice. Fragments, dialogue, repetition, dialect, compression, strangeness, humor, and roughness may be doing real work. Edit them only when they block the reader for the stated purpose.
- Treat "clearer" and "more professional" as scoped requests. They do not authorize flattening fiction, removing necessary qualifiers, rewriting unrelated sections, or changing the author's stance.
- For factual or sourced prose, keep claims inside the provided evidence. Preserve attribution and limits: if a source says participants reported fewer missed handoffs, do not turn that into a measured reduction, proof, or causal result. A citation, URL, or named source helps inspection; it does not make a claim true. Do not certify publication readiness unless the user explicitly asks for that decision and the evidence supports it.

## Small Example

If asked to make a suspense excerpt "clearer and more professional," first check whether the clipped rhythm is creating suspense. A useful answer may be: "No substantial reader-flow issue. I would keep the fragments and only clarify the one physical detail that may cause a pause."

## Common Mistakes

- Inventing a clarity problem because the prose is unusual.
- Starting with comma, tone, or polish comments when the real issue is unsupported evidence or reader orientation.
- Offering one polished rewrite when the writer asked for different audiences.
- Removing qualifiers such as "pilot," "internal," "reported," "early," or "not yet replicated."
