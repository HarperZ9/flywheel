# Research synthesis method

Flywheel uses this method for research write-ups and for an agent's own
synthesis work. Lilian Weng described seven steps for writing her Lil'Log posts
(lilianweng.github.io), and they form the structure here. Each step adds a
Flywheel requirement that keeps the difference between what a source shows and
what we infer visible.

## The seven steps

1. **Anchor on one clear research direction.** State the theme in one sentence
   before reading. Flywheel adds: name the decision or build the synthesis should
   inform, as the [evidence rule](evidence-and-useful-work.md) requires. If no
   decision would change, ask whether the work is worth doing.
2. **Read widely around the theme.** Cover the key results, including the ones
   that cut against the expected story. Flywheel adds: prefer primary sources.
   Record what could not be read, such as a paywall or a refused request, as a
   coverage gap, and never fill it from memory. Record the date each source was
   read.
3. **Organize what you read into a clear classification.** Build the taxonomy
   before writing prose. Flywheel adds: a source can sit in several categories.
   The taxonomy is a working tool, and it gets revised when a source does not fit.
4. **Extract the shared logic, core ideas or unified framework.** Find the thread
   that runs through the work. Flywheel adds: state the framework as mechanisms or
   equations that make predictions, so someone can test it. Note the sources that
   fit it badly, because new results tend to hide there.
5. **Step outside individual papers and reorganize around the distilled
   storyline.** Write around the framework and drop the paper-by-paper order.
   Flywheel adds: a reader must still be able to trace each claim to its source.
6. **Use your own understanding and derivations to fill gaps between studies.**
   Flywheel adds a binding requirement: label every gap-filling claim as inferred
   or proposed, keep it apart from what sources show, and give it the cheapest
   test that could check it. A derivation stays a hypothesis until someone checks
   it. This is the step where a synthesis most easily overclaims.
7. **Structure the result as a logically smooth, progressively layered
   argument.** A reader should be able to follow it from start to finish.
   Flywheel adds: lead with the result or the decision. Put the essential limit
   beside each claim. Keep honest nulls and dropped ideas in a section of their
   own.

## Limits

The method organizes a synthesis. A synthesis written this way is still only as
good as its sources and its checks, and the labels in step 6 are what keep a
reader from mistaking an inference for a finding.
