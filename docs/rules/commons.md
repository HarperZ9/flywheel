# The commons

This is the thesis the [just-culture rule](just-culture.md) serves, adopted on
October 2, 2026: protect the integrity of the commons. Flywheel exists to serve
it, and it binds the maintainer first.

The works cited below support the thesis. They do not prove it, and each one is
cited for the specific point beside it.

## 1. Protect the integrity of the commons

The commons here is the shared record that people and models use to decide what
is true and what to do: knowledge, evidence, measurements, public claims and the
accounting a community keeps. Concealment, a captured review and a gamed measure
each damage that record for everyone who relies on it. That is why the
just-culture rule ranks concealment above the original error.

## 2. Provenance recorded at the source, and reinspectable

Record provenance where the thing is made, and keep it open so anyone affected
can reinspect it. The practice is simple and old.

- The earliest writing was receipts. Proto-cuneiform tablets from Uruk, from
  about 3300 to 3000 BCE, are mostly accounts of goods, labor and debts.
- Socratic elenchus is a protocol for argument. Socrates does not ask the other
  person to trust him. He asks them to state what they believe, and the two
  examine it together, one step at a time, until both see where the reasoning
  holds and where it breaks. Plato's dialogues are the written receipts of those
  arguments.
- Flywheel carries the same practice into AI work: a receipt at the point of
  origin that a third party can re-derive.

As AI output fills the network, a person still has to judge quality, novelty and
accuracy, and whether a person or a model made a thing. That judgment gets
harder. Receipts at the origin keep it possible.

The maintainer also holds a personal observation, labeled as one: that
engagement-driven algorithms isolate people, sow division and wear down the drive
to seek answers, and that capital incentives reward the separation. The evidence
on this is mixed, and the label travels with the claim wherever it is cited.

## 3. Independence is structural

A review is independent when the party under review controls none of its
funding, appointments or access. Independence granted by permission fails,
because the party that grants it can narrow or withdraw it. The old rule is
*nemo iudex in causa sua*: no one judges their own cause.

Examples of the crack: a platform company studying its own effects on users while
its revenue depends on engagement, and governments reviewing their own conduct.

- Michael W. Wagner, "Independence by permission", *Science* (2023), his account
  as the independent rapporteur on the 2020 Meta election studies. The outside
  researchers' independence existed by the company's permission.
- Ibn Khaldun, *Muqaddimah* (1377). Group cohesion, *asabiyyah*, builds a
  dynasty. Rulers who set themselves apart from the group erode it, and the
  dynasty falls, often within a few generations. Reading this as support for
  structural independence is an interpretation.

In Flywheel, a review records the funding, appointment and access behind it
beside its findings. [Independence](../INDEPENDENCE.md) sets out the record an
external assessment should carry.

## 4. Independence serves reciprocity

The independence meant here is independence from capture. It serves mutual
understanding, care and reciprocity: care for the result, for the process, for
oneself, for others and for the earth. It excludes the kind of independence that
only reinforces one's own ideas, isolates people or uses others as a stepping
stone. There is no good ending for people without some form of reciprocity.

## 5. Participation and mutual monitoring

Everyone can see the accounting. Members help make the rules. Monitoring is
mutual, and it comes from the group coordinating, planning, discussing and
taking part. An outside arbiter or a position of authority does not impose it.
Participation is never minimized.

This fits with section 3. Structural independence says who may not control a
check: the party being checked. Participation says who makes the rules and sees
the accounting: the members. A check controlled by the party it checks and a rule
imposed by a party nobody can check fail the same way.

Mutual monitoring gets harder with scale: large and fast-growing populations, limited resources,
and conflicting cultural, national and personal interests. This section makes no
claim that mutual monitoring scales.

Elinor Ostrom, *Governing the Commons* (1990), found that commons that last let
the people affected make the rules, use monitors accountable to the members,
apply graduated sanctions and keep conflict resolution cheap.

## 6. Reintegration and a path back

Accountability comes with a path back. In a community where members monitor each
other, shame for misconduct lets a person take responsibility, learn and rejoin
after making changes. Nobody gets cast as a permanent victim or villain, and the
community can sustain and steward itself. Obligation 6 of the just-culture rule
is the working form inside Flywheel.

Debt is the oldest case. The first receipts recorded debts, and a record with no
release valve ends in bondage. The Sumerian *amargi*, often rendered "freedom",
was used for debt-release decrees in Lagash in the 24th century BCE; the
translation and dating carry moderate confidence.

John Braithwaite, *Crime, Shame and Reintegration* (1989): reintegrative shaming
condemns the act and opens a way back, and stigmatizing shame makes outcasts who
turn on the community.

## 7. Common ground with incumbents

The open question is how to make this worth it both to the people who would
benefit and to those who now hold the existing arrangement. Five levers work
together.

- **Aligned interest.** Show the incumbent what it gains: cheaper discovery of
  errors, lower liability and trust it can carry into markets. This is
  inference, with no cited source.
- **Repeated games.** Robert Axelrod, *The Evolution of Cooperation* (1984): in
  repeated interaction, reciprocal strategies that are nice, retaliatory,
  forgiving and clear outperform defection once the future matters enough.
- **Credible exit.** Albert O. Hirschman, *Exit, Voice, and Loyalty* (1970):
  members respond to decline by leaving or by speaking up, and voice carries
  weight when exit is credible. Portable, re-derivable receipts and
  vendor-neutral tools make exit credible. The application is inference.
- **Collective demand.** Users, buyers, insurers and regulators asking for the
  same receipts together change what incumbents supply. This is a design
  direction with no cited source.
- **A dignified way back, on condition of disclosure.** Concealment by those who hold
  power is the sanctioned case, and their way back requires full disclosure.
  South Africa's Truth and Reconciliation Commission granted amnesty for
  politically motivated acts only on full disclosure.

## Limits

- This is a design value and a framing for the work. It authorizes no action
  against any party, and it overrides no authorization, privacy, legal,
  disclosure, release or safety requirement.
- The [neutral-evaluation rule](evidence-and-useful-work.md#neutral-evaluation-of-models-and-organizations)
  still governs evaluation: the same criteria for every organization, Flywheel's
  own included. Naming an incumbent's structural conflict is a finding about
  structure and makes no claim about intent.
- Whether mutual monitoring scales is unknown.
